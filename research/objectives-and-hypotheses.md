# Objectives and hypotheses

## Objectives
1. Review feature-discovery and representation-steering approaches.
2. Identify candidate features associated with refusal / safety behavior.
3. Test candidate features using causal interventions and appropriate controls.
4. Evaluate targeted steering on safety and benign tasks.
5. Quantify collateral effects, uncertainty, and reproducibility.

## Research questions
- RQ1: How consistently can candidate safety-related features be identified across prompts and model contexts?
- RQ2: Which candidates demonstrate causal influence under ablation, activation patching, or other controlled interventions?
- RQ3: What is the trade-off between safety behavior and benign-task utility after steering?

## Provisional hypotheses
- H1: A subset of candidate features will show reproducible causal effects on refusal-related behavior.
- H2: Feature-targeted interventions may offer a more selective safety/utility trade-off than broad layer-level interventions.

These are hypotheses, not results. Keep, revise, or reject them based on the observed evidence.
